How to Make a WhatsApp Chat for Instagram Reels, Stories & Memes

A believable chat screenshot is one of the most scroll-stopping things you can put in a Reel, Story or meme. Instead of screenshotting a real conversation, build exactly the exchange you need — both sides, photos, blue ticks — and export it as a sharp image. Here’s the quick way, plus the little details that make it look real on video.

🎬 Make your chat screenshot

Why creators build chats instead of screenshotting

  • Privacy — you never have to expose a real person’s number, name or photo.
  • Control — write the exact punchline, set the perfect timing, no blurring or cropping.
  • Reusable — change one line and re-export for a whole series without hunting old screenshots.
  • Clean — no battery bar you forgot to hide, no other notifications sneaking into the frame.

Step by step

  1. Open Chat TreeGo to chattreeapp.fun and choose Create your chat — a blank WhatsApp-style conversation opens.
  2. Write the exchangeTurn on Edit, type each message and pick who is sending it. Build up to your punchline so the last visible bubble does the work.
  3. Add photos & reactionsDrop in a photo or two, quote a message as a reply, add emoji reactions, and set each of your messages to sent, delivered or double-blue read ticks.
  4. Match your videoSet the contact name and avatar, choose the phone frame and status bar, and switch light or dark mode to match your Reel’s background.
  5. Export the imageExport as a sharp PNG and drop it straight into your Reel, Story, meme or thumbnail.

Tips that make it look real on Reels & Stories

  • Leave room at top and bottom. Reels and Stories crop the edges and add UI — keep the key bubbles in the middle third of the frame.
  • Bigger is better. Fewer, larger messages read on a phone screen in a second; a wall of tiny text does not.
  • Use blue ticks with intent. “Delivered” grey ✓✓ but not read can be funnier or more dramatic than everything blue.
  • Mind the timing. Space a couple of replies a few minutes apart so it feels like a real back-and-forth, not a script.
  • Dark mode for night scenes. If your Reel background is dark, a dark chat blends in far better than a bright white one.
  • One photo sells it. A single image with a short caption breaks up the text and makes the scene believable.
